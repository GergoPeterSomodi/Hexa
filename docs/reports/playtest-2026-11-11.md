# Hexa playtest report: Age of Discovery (Portugal)

**Build tested:** last pushed `main` of GergoPeterSomodi/Hexa (commit 24f6089, version 0.1.1), cloned to a scratch folder. No code was changed.
**Method:** headless scripted play (SDL dummy driver, 1024x708 window), clicks and keys sent through the real event handler. Economy run of 40 seasons, a raise-and-invade war against Castile, a colony expedition, save/load, Esc menu, a random world, and an idle run to the 1650 end date.
**Screenshots:** taken locally during the playtest, not stored in the repo (short names below refer to them).

Caveat: the playtest is scripted, so "feel" is judged from the numbers, the screens and the log text, not from watching it move. Sound, animation and real mouse feel were not tested.

---

## 1. Verdict

- **Is it fun yet?** Not yet. The first season is pleasant and the map looks good, but from about season 10 the loop becomes "invest in a tile, end season" with no real choices left. Money piles up unused.
- **Best part:** the tile-art map with its fog, borders and stack badges, and the market panel. The market panel shows the profit of each trade order ("Export wine: Lisbon > London, +5.8 x 12 = +69") and is the most game-like decision screen in the build.
- **Biggest problem:** the economy has no pressure and no sink. An idle Portugal reaches 9,000+ ducats by 1517 with 27 provinces, and the best money lever (merchants) is hidden: 0 of 2 merchants used in my first 40 seasons, yet one wine route alone was worth about +69 a season against an income of about 50.
- **Second problem:** no stated goal. Nothing on the first screens says what winning or losing means, or how the game ends at 1650.
- **Third problem:** war is opaque. Armies have no odds or preview, battles show only one log line, and provinces flip owner while your own stack stands in them.

---

## 2. What works (keep these)

1. **Top bar.** Treasury with income per season (e.g. "177 ducats (+48.1/season)"), date, merchants free, and the End button in one row. Clear at a glance.
2. **Province panel.** People (peasants, burghers, nobles), food supply, what the tile produces and how much it is worth, then the tile, settlement, castle and building rows, each with its cost. Dense but well organised (`04_first_turn_portugal`, `07_province_panel_near_lisbon`).
3. **Investment on tiles.** Cheap (25-50 ducats), instant, and visible: the tile art changes and the log says "near Porto: now a open fields (+60% grain)". This is the right size of decision for a new player.
4. **Market panel.** A full price / supply / demand / trade table, with each trade order shown as profit per season, not just a price (`09_merchant_options`). This is the design to copy into other systems.
5. **Country chooser.** Country stats and a one-line flavour tip ("Best placed to sail around Africa and across the Atlantic") before you commit (`03_portugal_country_panel_chooser`).
6. **Ledger.** Clean ranking, your country highlighted in gold (`32_ledger_panel`).
7. **Esc menu.** Resume, Save, Load, Options, Main menu, Exit, with a hint strip for F5, F9 and F11 (`27_esc_menu_main`).
8. **Saving.** Quicksave (F5) and load (F9 or "Load game") restored the same treasury, turn and country. Autosave shows a toast and does not block play.
9. **Map and war readability.** Stacks show a number badge; borders are coloured per country; the war map reads at a glance (`15_war_turn_1`).
10. **Colonisation mechanics work end to end.** Raise colonists, board a caravel, sail into the fog, unload onto a free coast, press B to found a colony. The game was not broken anywhere along that path.
11. **Random world** starts with invented names and a sensible overview (`29_random_world_start`).
12. **Performance.** Europe loads in 1.7 s, a random world in 2.7 s. Ending a season takes 0.05-0.2 s early in the game and 0.3-0.4 s by 1510-1530. Redraw is 60-90 ms. No exceptions were raised in any run.

## 3. Problems found while playing

Bugs are marked **[bug]**, the rest are confusing or slow flows and design gaps.

### 3.1 Bugs and visible defects

1. **[bug] Invest button text overflows into its cost.** The label "Invest: Terraced vineyard" runs over the "49" cost on the button. Repro: play Portugal, click a farm-type province near Lisbon (e.g. the hex at 125,59), see the Invest row. Screenshot: `07_province_panel_near_lisbon`.
2. **[bug] Grammar in log messages.** "now a open fields", "now a olive trees", "now a upland grazing", "now a olive terraces". The article is always "a". Repro: invest in any grain or olive province. Screenshot: `econ_s20`.
3. **[bug, found by reading code] Export / Import order buttons are always enabled.** The code has `margin > 0 or True`, so an order with negative margin can still be given. My test only hit positive margins, so this is not yet seen in play.
4. **[bug] Freshly raised units freeze the whole stack.** A unit raised this season has 0 moves, and the stack moves at its slowest unit. Repro: raise a Knights in Porto where Pikemen already stand, click the stack, right-click a border hex. Result: reach is 1 hex and the toast says "They cannot get there." with no reason. Screenshot: `13_porto_stack_selected`.
5. **[bug / unclear] Provinces flip while your army stands in them.** Repro: declare war on Castile (`Diplomacy with Castile` on a border province, then `Declare war`), march a 2-unit stack into the border province. Next season the log says "Portugal took control of near Porto", then "Castile took control of near Porto" while the stack is still there. Two seasons later there is "Battle at near Porto: Castile lost 21 strength, Portugal lost 8" in the log, and the stack is "cut off from supply". The map never shows who won. Screenshots: `14_porto_stack_ordered`, `15_war_turn_1`.
6. **[bug, minor] Toasts hide hover hints.** Autosave (and any toast) takes priority over the bottom-left hint for 3.5 s, so hovering a top-bar button shows "Autosaved." instead of its tooltip. Screenshot: `33_hover_card_markets_button`.
7. **[minor] "-0.0" shown** for Young colonies in the country panel when there are none. Screenshot: `04_first_turn_portugal`.

### 3.2 Confusing UI

8. **No stated goal.** The start banner says "Choose your country: click any country on the map". The first message says "Grow your economy, trade, and reach for the New World." Nothing says how the game is won, what the score means, or that it ends in 1650. The ledger shows a "Score" column with no explanation (`32_ledger_panel`).
9. **"Markets" and "Market" in the top bar.** "Markets" is the market map mode; "Market" opens the home market panel. Next to each other they read as duplicates.
10. **Ten buttons all called "Build".** In a province panel, every building row says "Build" and the building name is just text to the left. Disabled buttons show the cost as their hint ("Costs 280 ducats") rather than why they are disabled, except for a few cases ("Needs about 2k burghers to staff it").
11. **Clicking a selected stack deselects it.** Clicking a unit you already have selected opens the province panel instead. Right-clicking a hex that is off-screen silently clears the selection. Repro: select the Porto stack, click it again, see the province panel.
12. **Passengers are hidden.** To land a colonist you must deselect the ship first, click it again, then pick the passenger from its panel. The "Found colony" button appears only in the unit panel, and only the B key was obvious. Roughly 12 clicks and 3 seasons for the first colony.
13. **Province names are generic.** Most provinces are called "near Lisbon", "near Porto", "near Seville", which makes the map hard to read and the log meaningless ("near Porto: now a wheat strips").
14. **Price colours are red and green only** in the market table (red = dear, green = cheap), with no legend and no shape cue. Also fails colour-blind players.
15. **Recruit stats are cryptic.** "Pikemen 4/5 mv 6" has no label for the 4 and the 5 (attack/defence?). Hover card probably explains, but the raw label does not.
16. **Province names clipped on the right edge** and "Sevilla" label cut by the panel at the edge of the map (minor).

### 3.3 Slow or tedious flows

17. **Dead time with money piling up.** My Portugal had nothing to invest in from about season 32. Treasury went 117 -> 314 over six seasons with no actions. A player doing nothing reached 9,053 ducats by 1517. Money has no sink, so the next choice is always "end season".
18. **Merchants are invisible.** "Merchants 0/2" sits in the top bar in grey, and nothing prompts the player to use them. The top route (Lisbon -> London, wine) is +69 a season, more than total income. Most players will never find it.
19. **One job per province per season.** Raising four troops takes four seasons in four provinces, and a province that builds or invests cannot raise a unit in the same season. This is a real constraint but is not explained until you try it ("This province already did its job this season").
20. **Tax has no visible trade-off.** "High" tax took income from 50.2 to 62.8 a season at once. The cost ("people grow slower") is only in a tooltip, so High looks like a free +12.6 a season.
21. **Fund research is poor value.** "Fund research: +15" for 60 ducats, against 160 points needed for the next era (not before 1520). The player cannot see whether this is good or bad.
22. **Exploration reveals little.** A caravel with 20 moves a season crossed to the fog edge in one season, but the known area only grew from 10,154 to 10,357 hexes. Discovery feels like little reward.
23. **War starts with no warning.** Declaring war on Castile was allowed at army 4.5 (Portugal) against 4.3 (Castile) with no odds shown. The button says "Armies can then enter their land and occupy provinces" and nothing else.
24. **Peace is unexplained.** Offering white peace got "They refuse: they are winning, or the war has only just started". There is no way to see what would be accepted.
25. **[bug / clarity] Game-over banner is clipped.** The end-of-game banner runs off the left edge ("has come. Final ranking: Mamluks, France, Ottomans lead. Press L for the ledger."). The player is not named in the ranking, so a player can finish the game without knowing their own result. Screenshot: `30_game_over`.
26. **Wars started by the AI are quiet.** In the idle run, Castile and Portugal were at war and peace arrived as one log line: "Peace between Castile and Portugal: 15 provinces changed hands." There is no alert when a war starts, no sign of who gained, and no chance to respond. Repro: play Portugal and end seasons without acting, then watch the log around 1600-1650.

## 4. Improvement ideas, ranked by impact / effort

Each entry: **what**, **why** (principle and an example game), **in Hexa** (where it appears).

### Quick wins (a day or less each)

**Q1. Fix the visible bugs (3.1 items 1-7).**
Why: Steam-readiness and trust. Any shipped strategy game (Old World, EU4) has clean text and no overflow.
In Hexa: wrap invest labels or shorten them ("Invest: Terraced vines"); add an article helper for log lines ("a" / "an" / no article for mass nouns); make the Export/Import buttons respect margin; add the "-0.0" guard.

**Q2. Name every province.**
Why: identity and readability. EU4 gives every province a name; Civ VI gives every city one. "near Porto" is noise.
In Hexa: use the real name of the nearest town plus a suffix ("Porto countryside"), or generate short region names from the terrain ("Douro hills", "Alentejo plains"). Shows in the province card title, the log and the market table.

**Q3. Give alerts their own space.**
Why: information hierarchy. Civ VI and Old World keep a status line for system messages and a separate tooltip layer.
In Hexa: keep toasts in the top bar or the log only, so hover hints at the bottom-left always show. Autosave goes to the log.

**Q4. Say why a button is disabled.**
Why: clear feedback. Civ VI and Old World show the blocking reason in the tooltip for every greyed-out action.
In Hexa: a disabled "Build" should say "Needs 2k free burghers (Lisbon has 0)" or "This province already did its job this season", not the cost.

**Q5. Rename the two market buttons.**
Why: label clarity. Two adjacent controls with near-identical names confuse first-time players.
In Hexa: "Markets" (map mode) becomes "Market map"; "Market" (panel) becomes "Trade" or "Open market". Also consider a "Trade" button only when a market is selected.

**Q6. Keep a stack selected when clicked again.**
Why: consistent selection. Civ VI and EU4 keep the unit selected; deselect is Esc.
In Hexa: clicking the selected stack does nothing; Esc deselects. Right-clicking off-map shows "Off the map" instead of clearing silently.

**Q7. Colour-blind cue for prices.**
Why: accessibility. Non-colour cues are a standard requirement (WCAG 1.4.1 "use of colour").
In Hexa: add ▲ / ▼ arrows next to the Price column and a small legend under the market title.

**Q8. Show the tax trade-off.**
Why: meaningful choices (Sid Meier). A choice only matters if both sides are visible. EU4 shows the stability and growth cost of each tax setting.
In Hexa: under Tax level, show "High: +12.6/season, people grow 25% slower" and "Low: +0 income, growth +25%". Do this from the same numbers the game already computes.

**Q9. Explain recruit stats.**
Why: progressive disclosure. Show the labels once and keep the short form.
In Hexa: "Pikemen  attack 4 / defence 5  moves 6" on first view, short form after the first recruit.

### Medium (a few days each)

**M1. An outliner / to-do panel (press T).**
Why: the EU4 outliner and Old World's advisor exist to stop the player hunting for idle things. Pacing and downtime (also Civ VI "next unit" cycling).
In Hexa: a collapsible list on the left edge: "Idle provinces: 6 (Porto, Lisbon...)", "Merchants free: 2 (open market)", "Food short: none", "Built this season: Weaver in Lisbon". Each row jumps the camera to the item. Turns "end season" into a checklist, and makes the empty seasons visible as "nothing left to do" rather than a stall.

**M2. Merchant onboarding and a suggested order.**
Why: hidden levers lose players. Civ VI's "Advisor" recommendations and Old World's suggested actions make the best options visible without automating them.
In Hexa: the first time a merchant is free, a banner says "Your merchant could earn +69 a season: Export wine, Lisbon > London. Send?" with one click. Later, show the best route in the Merchants counter tooltip. Keep the decision with the player.

**M3. Surplus sink and rising costs.**
Why: anti-hoarding and pacing. Civ VI's wonders and EU4's idea costs absorb money; EU4 raises the cost of expansion as you grow.
In Hexa: a "Crown project" list (cathedral, university, royal road, navy yard) that uses surplus ducats for long-term bonuses, plus a visible cost rise for each new province. Treasury above about 300 shows a suggestion.

**M4. Army odds and a battle preview.**
Why: interesting decisions and fair feedback. Civ VI shows combat odds before you attack; EU4 shows the army comparison before war.
In Hexa: on Declare war and on hover of an enemy stack, show "Your 4.5 vs their 4.3, win chance about 50%". On attack, show the expected result.

**M5. A battle report.**
Why: juice and readability. Total War and Civ VI show a result card with each side's losses; Old World shows a combat summary.
In Hexa: after a battle, a small card in the log area and a pop on the hex: "Battle at near Porto: you won, Castile lost 21 strength, you lost 8 (1 unit destroyed)". Clicking it centres the map on the fight.

**M6. Fix the occupation rule and show it.**
Why: a rule the player cannot see feels like a bug. Show who controls a province and why.
In Hexa: when a province changes hands, the card says "Held by Castile: your stack was cut off from supply" or "Your 2 units left, Castile walked in". Also show a supply indicator on stacks.

**M7. Fresh recruits can move (or say so).**
Why: avoid a silent freeze. Old World's new units show "ready next turn" on the unit card.
In Hexa: show "Ready next season" on the stack card and in the reach highlight; or let a fresh recruit leave the stack alone.

**M8. Colonisation: one button to land and found.**
Why: fewer clicks in a flow that is already complex. Civ VI's settler "found city" from an adjacent tile is a single action.
In Hexa: when a colonist is on a ship next to free coast, the ship panel shows "Land colonists here (and found)". Passengers are listed in the ship panel with their moves.

**M9. Exploration reward.**
Why: discovery feels good only when it pays. Civ VI gives a reward for exploring unknown tiles (goody huts, contacts).
In Hexa: each newly sighted coast or native village gives a small payoff (a map reveal, a gold or food bonus, or a contact with the local tribe). Log it: "Discovered the Azores: +40 ducats".

### Big (a week or more)

**B1. Goals and campaign phases.**
Why: short, medium and long-term goals (EU4 missions and national ideas; Civ VI's victory tracks). Without them, "end season" has no direction.
In Hexa: an "Objectives" card (press O) with short goals ("Build a town in Lisbon by 1485") and a medium one per era ("1520: 40 provinces, Discovery of the Gold Coast"), plus a visible end-game score line. Show the score breakdown in the ledger's Score column.

**B2. A visible end and a score.**
Why: a game needs a clear ending to be worth finishing (CK3's dynasty legacy screen; Old World's final score).
In Hexa: a 1650 end card with the score breakdown (provinces, trade, colonies, prestige) and a "what would have won" line. This also gives the player something to aim at.

**B3. Smarter rivals.**
Why: difficulty and AI that feels fair (Old World, EU4). The AI hoards too: the Ottomans ended with 2,876 ducats and no visible plan.
In Hexa: AI that spends its money on the same sinks as the player (M3), makes alliances and trade deals, and reacts when you grow.

**B4. Scripted first season (onboarding).**
Why: progressive disclosure and an EU4-style tutorial. Civ VI and Old World both teach by doing.
In Hexa: a first-season checklist that highlights the top bar, one investment, one build, one merchant order, and the End button, with an option to skip. Each step unlocks the next panel.

**B5. Animation and sound hooks.**
Why: juice. Units sliding, a battle flash, coin pops on income. Humankind and Old World use all three to make turns feel rewarding.
In Hexa: floating "+12" over the treasury on income, a short move slide for stacks (partly there already, `animate`), and sound hooks on invest, battle, and season end.

**B6. Steam polish pass.**
Why: Steam expectations: text size, colour-blind palette, remappable keys, settings, achievements, stable save.
In Hexa: a text-size option in Options, a colour-blind palette, key remapping in the Esc menu, and achievements tied to the objectives in B1.

## 6. Crashes and reproducible bugs (summary)

- No crashes or exceptions in any run: the 40-season economy, the war, the colony expedition, save and load, the Esc menu, the random world, and a full idle run from 1480 to the end at 1650.
- Reproducible bugs: 3.1 items 1, 2, 4, 5 and 25 (steps given above). Item 3 is from code reading.

**Full idle run (no player actions after the first 40 seasons):** game over reached at season 684 (Spring 1651), in 279 s total, worst season 0.88 s. Treasury grew to 76,374 ducats with income at +171 a season, and Portugal (27 provinces) still did not make the top three. The Ottomans ended with about 2,900 ducats at 1523, so the AI also hoards rather than spends. The clear implication: an idle player wins on money alone, which is the "no sink" problem in 3.2-17 and idea M3.

## 7. Notes for the owner

- Headless window is 1024x708 because the dummy display reports that size, so some label overflow may look different on a 1280x720 or 1920x1080 window. The overflow in 3.1-1 was seen at 1024 width.
- Hexa's own tests were not run.
- The playtest used only the scripted UI paths. Hover tooltips on the top bar were not confirmed working (see 3.1-6).
