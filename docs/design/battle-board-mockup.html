The Battle Board

Your sketch, made playable: three lanes, a front and a back row, a reserve, one die a side and a hand of cards. Play a battle below, then read what I would add to make every day of it a real decision.

Each day of a battle, the enemy shows its plan, you answer it with a few command points and cards, then the dice fall. Five days at most, about a minute a battle.

What your sketch already gets right

  • Lanes, rows and a reserve match the battle Hexa already has (M7: three lanes and a reserve a side) plus the back slots you approved on 13 November.
  • One die and named modifiers at the top. That is the "fortune of the day" the 13 November review measured as the fix for battles that were either a coin flip or a sure thing.
  • The day track with crossed boxes. A battle you can see the end of is easy to read.
  • Cards at the bottom, as approved in rounds 3 to 9 of docs/design/battle.md.

What is missing

  • A reason to act on a given day. If the units fight on their own and the cards are boosts, the best play is "play the biggest card". The enemy's plan, shown first, gives each day a question.
  • Decisions without cards. Until the tech tree is in Godot (M11 builds no battle cards) the board needs its own levers: moving regiments, the reserve, withdrawing.
  • A way to skip it. A country at war fights several battles a season. The board must be optional for small ones.

Ten ideas, in four groups

Marked pitch are the ones I would build first; the mockup plays them. The rest are options.

A · The day loop

1. Show their plan first

pitch

Your second sketch becomes the screen. Before you act, each enemy regiment's target for today is drawn as a red arrow. You answer it: shore up the lane they mass on, pull a gun out of reach, feint where they are thin. Without it, cards on an auto-battle are guesses; with it, every day is a small puzzle.

From: Into the Breach and Slay the Spire, which show what the enemy will do and let you respond. Cost: small. The plan is a pure function of the board, redrawn after every move.

2. One die a side, modifiers in the open

pitch

As in the sketch: d6 plus named chips, total against total. Hover a chip to see where it comes from: a tech, an idea, the ground, the general. The luck sits in one visible roll a day, so a close fight swings and a 2-to-1 fight does not.

From: the Europa Universalis battle window; Hexa's own measurements (per-hit luck averaged out to nothing). Cost: none in the rules. M7 already rolls a d6 a side a day; this only shows it.

3. Five days and one morale bar

option

Your crossed-box day track, and a single tug-of-war bar instead of numbers. At the last day, the side with lower morale withdraws (M7's rule; M7 caps at 8 days, the board would want 5 so a battle stays short).

Cost: a data change and a balance check with the scenario bands.

B · Where the tactics come from

4. A targeting rule you can read

pitch

Front regiments hit the lane opposite. If its front is empty, they break through to its back row; if that is empty too, they swing into the next lane as a flank (×1.25). Crossbows shoot their own lane, then a neighbour; guns reach any lane. So leaving a lane thin has a price you can see in the arrows.

From: Darkest Dungeon, where where a unit stands decides what it can hit. Close to M7's flank rule.

5. Command points from the general

pitch

Three a day, a good general one more. Spend them on moving a regiment to another lane, sending in the reserve, rotating a worn regiment back (your rotation idea of 13 November), or a card. One currency for everything: a country with no techs still has choices, and techs add options rather than being the only source of them.

From: commanders in Crusader Kings III and Hearts of Iron IV, whose skill sets how much they can do.

6. Deploy before day one

option

A short deploy step for both sides, with the presets you approved in round 2 (Pike and shot, Cavalry wings, Refused flank, Gun line). The 13 November review found the defender had no say before day 1 when the AI attacks; this gives it one.

Cost: one more screen; skip it with "use my preset".

C · Cards, rethought

7. Cards come from your regiments

pitch

Pikemen bring Pike hedge, knights a Knightly charge, guns a Grand battery. A tech upgrades a card (Pike hedge to Pike square) or adds one (Countermarch, Feigned retreat). The general brings one card of his own. You can predict your hand from your army, so building an army on the map becomes building a deck. The deck stays at 8 to 15 cards, and the board works before the tech tree is in Godot.

From: Marvel Snap's 12-card decks; Gwent's rows. Your approved card list from rounds 3 and 4 fits this almost as it is.

8. Hidden cards and a reason to scout

option

Sneaky cards lie face down on a lane until they fire (approved in round 3). In the mockup Milan lays one on day 2. A Scouts card, or a cavalry general, turns one over. Show how many cards the enemy holds, so a bluff reads as a bluff.

Cost: the AI needs a rule for when to bluff; start with "never".

Cards (recommended)

Draw one a day, hold at most five. Surprise and stories ("the feigned retreat on day 3"). Keep the hand small so most of the luck stays in the one die.

Or: stratagems

A fixed menu of 6 to 8 orders unlocked by techs, each once a battle, no draw. Easier to balance and for the AI to play; but the best order is the same every time, and there is less to tell afterwards.

D · Keeping it light

9. Watch, command or auto

pitch

The window opens only for battles with a side of yours, by a setting: always, big battles only, never. Auto lets your general fight it by his traits (the mockup's "Let the general command"). Battles between AI countries stay instant.

For: fun principle 11, readable and light to run.

10. A one-glance result

option

After the battle: killed and wounded each side, the deciding moment ("your Grand battery on day 3"), experience gained, where the loser went. One card, one click back to the map.

Cost: small; M7's battle record already holds the numbers.

Rules for the minimal view

  • Seven rows and nothing else on the board: their reserve, back, front, the gap, your front, back, reserve.
  • A regiment is a box: two letters for its kind, a bar for its men. Colour means side, never kind.
  • Only today's arrows. Theirs solid red; yours faint gold; flanks dashed.
  • Dice and modifiers in the top corners; day track and morale bar between them.
  • Cards at the bottom: name, cost, one line of rules, where it came from. The full card on hover.
  • Exact men, morale per regiment and the full log on hover or in the log line, never on the board.
  • The boxes are small on purpose: you decided width is 3 to 7 lanes, and seven lanes of 34-pixel boxes still fit a 1280-wide window.

How it fits the Godot plan

  • M7 builds the battle as one pure function, Combat.fight, settled inside the battles phase, with records the battle dock plays back. Its spec already names "the battle board" as its own milestone after M12, with Combat.fight and the records as the seam. This pitch is that milestone; it changes nothing in M7.
  • The board steps the fight a day at a time (Combat.day), with your moves and cards as part of the input. Same seed and same orders give the same battle, so tests and replays still work.
  • End season pauses in the battles phase only for a battle with a human side. Cards and command points are definitions (.tres) like every other number.
  • One question for you: the board as a pause inside End season (my pick: it keeps your 0.2.1 rule that a battle ends within the turn), or battles that carry over one day per season.
