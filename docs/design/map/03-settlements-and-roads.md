# Thread 3: settlements and roads (draft, 2026-11-14)

Game designer, design thread 3 of the map design (`00-brief.md`). **Status: draft for the owner. Nothing is built, no code
was changed.** Mockups: `03_prototype_network.png` (the prototype region worked by hand) and `03b_levels_sites_sockets.png`
(how a city levels up, where places sit and why, the sockets). Relief and water in the mockups are stand-ins drawn by hand
(threads 1 and 2 own the real ones); the sites, roads, bridges and passes are this thread's model.

## 1. The model in five lines

1. **People live where land, water and trade meet:** river crossings, confluences, bays, valley mouths, pass feet, lake
   shores. Each hex gets a site score; the real cities are fixed, everything else is placed by that score and a spacing rule.
2. **A settlement is a level, not a picture:** level 0 to 5 (nothing, hamlet, village, town, city, great city), a capital
   flag, a castle level 0 to 3, and at most one landmark (hospice, monastery, mine). The picture is a pure function of
   (level, edge sockets, ground, features), so a level-up just re-picks the picture.
3. **Roads are routed, not collapsed:** a graph of who must connect to whom (capital to cities, cities to towns, along
   valleys, over passes, along coasts) is routed over the relief with a cost, valley floors cheap, ridges closed except at
   passes, river crossings dear unless a place can build a bridge. Each hex edge then carries a road quality 0 to 3.
4. **Crossings are typed:** ford, wooden bridge, stone bridge, ferry, or pass, chosen by river size, road quality and the
   level of the place beside it. Ports get a quay on the sea, lake or river edge.
5. **Fields and suburbs spill, they are not tiles:** a derived, cosmetic layer around towns, scattered on a world grid so it
   runs across hex edges, never saved.

## 2. Goal (what the player sees and why it is fun)

The player reads the map like a trader or a general: a city sits where a road meets a river, a pass is a thin brown line
that ends at a hospice, a great city throws its roads and suburbs out into the plain, a ferry is the weak link on a Po
crossing. "Where do I march" and "where do I trade" are answered by the picture. Growing a town is visible: the same hex gets
a wall, a gate on every road, a stone bridge. The map tells the history of 1480 without a manual.

## 3. The fixed skeleton and what is generated

| Layer | Fixed (from `godot/data/geography`) | Generated by this thread |
|---|---|---|
| Cities | 102 `cities` with a size (0 to 250): position, name, rank. Level from size (section 5). | Snap to the best land hex within 1 hex of the true position (never on water). |
| Other places | `placenames` (6,940 names, biggest first; a hex shows the best three). | A place's level from its rank in that list. Villages and hamlets with no name are generated and named from the region's name pool. |
| Passes | 24 named `passes` plus the 72 pass edges the game derives. | Hospice on the pass hex; the track over it; the pass road's two approach valleys. |
| Regions, countries | `regions`, `countries`: who owns what in 1480. | Nothing. Roads cross borders; owners only change colour. |
| Rivers, mountains, coasts | `rivers`, `terrain_shapes`, `land` (threads 1 and 2 turn these into relief and water). | Nothing; this thread reads relief and water. |
| Castles | The Python rule: castle level from city size. | Standalone castles at chokepoints (pass foot, bridgehead, valley mouth). |
| Roads, bridges, ferries, quays | None in the data. | All of it, from the graph and the routing. |
| Monasteries, mines | A short authored list (section 12, question 3). | The rest, from relief and ore. |

**Gap found in the data:** `placenames` lacks Como, Monza, Varese, Susa, Brig and Airolo (the pruning dropped places near a
bigger neighbour). Milan's hinterland and the pass towns are exactly what this region needs, so a small authored "notable
places" file is required (question 3), not optional.

## 4. Scale: the finding that decides the rest

The existing grid (`SCALE = 2`) has a hex about 55 km across. Counting 43 real places of the prototype region:

| Grid | Hex width | Hexes holding the 43 places | Worst hex |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today (`SCALE 2`) | about 55 km | 25 | Milan, Como, Monza, Lecco and Varese in one hex |
| Half size (`SCALE 4`) | about 28 km | 37 (after snapping; 6 hexes share two places) | Milan and Monza |

At 55 km a "city with suburbs spilling into its neighbours" is impossible, because its neighbours are the next city, and an
Alpine valley (3 to 5 km wide) is invisible. At 28 km Pavia, Lodi, Como, Monza and Bergamo each get a hex, a valley is a
one-hex ribbon, and Milan to Pavia is one or two hexes (the grid quantises 30 km to 1 or 2 rows). The mockups use 28 km.
The model is written with the places-per-hex count as a parameter, so it also works at 55 km (up to 3 places per hex, the
best one drawn, the others as satellite names, as `naming.py` does today). The choice belongs to the owner with threads 1
and 4 (question 1). Cost: about 4 times the hexes (Europe about 122,000 instead of 30,000), which thread 4 must check.

## 5. Settlement kinds and levels

Level ladder (the tile art is mockup `08_closeup_upgrades.png`, town row; the ladder is in `03b` panel A):

| Level | Name | Looks like | Rule of thumb in 1480 | From a city `size` | From a `placenames` rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | none | countryside | | | |
| 1 | Hamlet | 2 to 4 houses | 100 to 500 people | generated | unnamed or rank above 4,000 |
| 2 | Village | church, green, fields | market village | size below 15 | rank 1,200 to 4,000 |
| 3 | Town | palisade, one gate per road | market town, a bishop's seat | 15 to 39 | rank below 1,200 |
| 4 | City | stone wall, towers, cathedral | a regional capital | 40 to 99 | authored upgrade |
| 5 | Great city | double wall, suburbs | Milan, Venice, Paris | 100 and above | |

The thresholds are data (`godot/data/settlement_levels.json`, proposed), not code. In the mockup, Genoa (60) is level 4, Turin
(15) level 3, Bern (5) level 2. Level is the visual scale; the game's economy still reads population (Python `SETTLEMENT_POP`:
village 6, town 20, city 45 thousand), mapped level to level by the economy thread.

Other kinds, all as flags on a hex, never as extra hexes:

| Kind | Where it sits | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital** | one per country, always a level 3 or more; a crown on the plate | Roads start here. |
| **Port** | coast hex with at least one sea edge; quay on that edge; a bay scores higher | Trade routes, ships. Lake ports (Como, Lecco, Arona) and river landings (Po, Ticino) use the same quay socket on a lake or river edge. |
| **Castle** (0 to 3) | with a town, or alone at a chokepoint: pass foot, bridgehead, valley mouth | Tower, keep, star fort. Standalone ones keep 3 hexes apart. |
| **Hospice** | on every pass hex (Great St Bernard, Simplon, Gotthard, Mont Cenis, Splügen) | A shelter on the climb; draws the pass name. |
| **Monastery** | quiet ground, about one per 6 settled hexes (Certosa di Pavia, Sacra di San Michele, Bobbio) | Flavour, later a small economy hook. |
| **Mine** | mountain or hills beside a valley road, over ore | Visual ladder in mockup 08; the economy thread decides output. |

A hex holds at most one settlement level, one castle level and one landmark. A second real place in the same hex is a
named satellite (Milan + Monza in the mockup).

## 6. Where a place sits (site rules)

Each land hex gets a score for "a settlement of level L could grow here" (`03b` panel C draws the six rules):

| Feature | Score | Examples in the region |
|---|---|---|
| River crossing: a bridge or ford saves a detour | +3 | Pavia (Ticino), Lodi (Adda), Piacenza (Po) |
| Confluence of two rivers | +4 | Turin (Po and Dora), Alessandria (Tanaro and Bormida) |
| Bay: sea on 2 or more sides, hills behind | +4 | Genoa |
| Valley mouth: a mountain valley opens on the plain | +3 | Como, Bergamo, Ivrea, Domodossola |
| Pass foot: the last flat hex before a pass | +3 | Aosta, Susa, Bellinzona |
| Lake shore harbour | +2 | Como, Arona, Lecco |
| Arable plain (fertility from thread 1) | +1 to +2 | the whole Po plain |
| Next to an existing road | +1 | |
| High mountain | -6 | |
| Marsh | -3 | |
| Ridge edge on the hex | -2 | |
| Within the spacing of a town of equal or higher level | -4 | see below |

**Spacing** (in hexes, from a place of the same or higher level; `SPACING` in the data file): level 5: 6; level 4: 4;
level 3: 3 (2 for fixed places such as Milan and Pavia); level 2: 2; level 1: 1. The Po plain gets a density bonus (a
village every 2 hexes), valleys and hills half of it, high mountains only the valley floors.

Fixed cities do not use the score to exist, only to snap: the best-scoring land hex within 1 hex of the true position,
ties kept where they are. This is why Genoa lands on the bay, not on the sea.

## 7. How a settlement looks and spills

- **In its hex:** the town picture sits beside the river and the road junction, not on the river (as in mockup 07). Every
  road socket becomes a street to the square; from level 3 each road passes a gate; from level 4 the gates are towers in
  the wall.
- **Into the neighbours** (`03b` panel B): a derived layer. Ring 1 (the six neighbours) gets field patches on arable hexes
  and, for levels 4 and 5, houses along the roads leaving the city (ribbon suburbs). Ring 2 gets a patch for level 5 only.
  Counts: level 3: 2 patches; level 4: 3 patches, orchards; level 5: 4 patches, 3 suburb houses per road. They are scattered
  on a world grid across hex edges, like trees and peaks in mockup 07, so fields never stop at an edge. They hold no
  state.
- **The map at far zoom:** a settlement is one plate with its level dots and name (levels 3 and up named); roads show as
  quality-coloured lines.

### How a city levels up visually (ties to the upgrade tiles)

The tile at level n is drawn from the same function as level n+1 with fewer parts (the owner's interest in upgrade tiles,
`docs/design/godot-look/08_closeup_upgrades.png`). Rules that keep it clean:

1. **Sockets never move.** A level-up changes the picture inside the hex and can raise a road's quality by one step; it never
   adds or removes a road socket, so neighbours need no redraw and no WFC re-run.
2. **One new feature per level:** 2 church, 3 palisade and gates, 4 stone wall and cathedral, 5 double wall and suburbs. A
   castle's ladder is separate (tower, keep, star fort).
3. **The surroundings answer:** a level-up changes ring 1 and ring 2 (more fields, suburbs), which is cosmetic only.
4. **Capital** adds a crown and a palace roof at any level 3 or more.

## 8. Roads

### Which places connect (the graph, in priority order)

1. **Capital to every city (level 4 or 5) of its own country**, and to each neighbouring capital within 12 hexes.
2. **City to the nearest two cities** of any country within 10 hexes (so Milan reaches Pavia, Lodi, Novara, Como, Bergamo).
3. **Each town (level 3) to the nearest town or city already on the network** (a minimum spanning tree).
4. **Each village (level 2) to the nearest road within 2 hexes** by a spur; hamlets get a track only if within 1 hex.
5. **Passes:** every pass joins the nearest place on each side (Como, Lugano, Bellinzona, Airolo, Gotthard; Novara, Arona,
   Domodossola, Simplon, Brig; Ivrea, Aosta, Great St Bernard, Martigny; Turin, Susa, Mont Cenis).
6. **Coasts:** every port to the next port along the shore (Genoa to Savona and to Spezia: the Riviera road).
7. **Valleys:** a mine or hospice connects to the nearest road along its valley.

This is the Python `build_roads` idea (towns to the nearest town on the network, capitals to neighbouring capitals) with two
changes: it joins across borders, and it adds the pass, coast and valley rules.

### How roads follow relief

Routing is a shortest path (Dijkstra on the hex graph) with these step costs, all data (`ROAD_COST`):

| Entering | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Plain | 1.0 | |
| Hills | 1.8 | |
| Valley floor (mountain hex with a valley flag) | 1.6 | The valley rule: roads climb along rivers. |
| Mountain | 3.6 | |
| High mountain | 8 | Almost never chosen. |
| Pass hex | 3.0 | |
| Ridge edge | closed | Open only where the edge carries a pass. |
| Sea, lake | closed | Roads follow the shore. |
| Edge already carrying a road | 0.35 | So routes share roads and trunks form. |
| River crossing | +0.4 stream, +0.6 or +2 river (cheap beside a place of level 2 or more), +1 or +4 great river (cheap beside a town) | This is why roads bunch at bridges. |

**Switchbacks:** a road hex on steep ground (mountain or high, not a valley floor) draws zigzags; a valley floor draws a
smooth curve. It needs only the hex's terrain and the two road edges, no extra socket.

### Road quality (0 to 3)

Quality is a property of an edge. Start: the highest rank among the links that use it (capital to city 3, city to town 2,
the rest 1). Then: the number of links sharing it (traffic 4 or more: one step up) and a cap by ground (mountain and high:
at most 2; a pass: 1 in 1480). In `03_prototype_network.png`: 14 highway edges, 27 road, 24 track.

| Level | Name | Shown | In 1480 | Proposed march cost (tuned by systems design) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | none | | | terrain cost |
| 1 | Track | thin dashed brown | mule and foot, no carts | 0.75 of terrain |
| 2 | Road | tan | carts, slow guns | 0.5 (the Python road value) |
| 3 | Highway | pale stone, dark casing | paved, convoys, guns | 0.35 |

### Bridges, fords, ferries

Chosen after routing, per road edge that crosses a river edge (thread 2's river classes: stream, river, great river):

| River | Road track (1) | Road (2) | Highway (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stream | ford | wooden bridge | wooden bridge |
| River | ford if small place, else wooden bridge | wooden bridge | stone bridge if a city (level 4 or more) is beside it |
| Great river (Po) | ferry | wooden or boat bridge if a town (level 3+) is beside it, else ferry | same (no stone bridge on the Po in 1480) |

A ferry is a weak link: slow (one hex of extra cost), one army at a time (capacity is the economy and war threads'), and
the first thing a navy cuts. A bridge is a chokepoint: a castle is likely beside it (the standalone castle rule).
Known weakness the prototype exposed: the rule is rank-based, so Pavia's level pays for a Po bridge although in history that
crossing was a ferry. The authored list (question 3) can override a crossing.

## 9. The prototype region worked by hand

`03_prototype_network.png`: 180 hexes at 28 km, 37 placed settlements (43 real places; 6 are satellites), 58 links, 54
nodes, 65 road edges. All links reached their destination (no unreachable pair).

What the hand-work showed:

- **Milan (great city, capital, castle 3)** is the star: highways to Pavia, Lodi (then Piacenza and Parma on the Via Emilia),
  Novara (then Vercelli and Turin), Monza, Como. Monza shares its hex as a named satellite. Suburbs run out along the roads.
- **Pavia (city, castle 2)** sits at the Ticino crossing: the only stone bridge of the region, on the Milan to Genoa trunk
  (Pavia, Voghera, Tortona, Novi, Giovi pass, Genoa). The Certosa stands one hex north.
- **Genoa (city, port, castle 2)** sits on the one bay; a quay edge into the sea; the Riviera coast road runs east to Rapallo
  and Spezia and west to Savona, Albenga, Imperia (the `Cadibona` and `Giovi` passes are generated, flagged on the map).
- **Turin (town, castle 2)** at the Po and Dora Riparia confluence, with a wooden Po bridge; the Mont Cenis track leaves
  along the Dora Riparia to Susa and the hospice.
- **The three passes of the brief:** *Gotthard* (Como, Lugano, Bellinzona on the Ticino valley, Airolo, a track over the
  pass and a hospice), *Simplon* (Novara, Arona on the lake shore, Domodossola in the Toce valley, hospice, Brig), *Great St
  Bernard* (Turin, Ivrea, Aosta in the Dora Baltea valley, hospice, Martigny). Passes are quality 1; the valley approaches
  are 1 to 2. Splügen, Mont Cenis, Larche, Tende, Cisa, Giovi and Cadibona are drawn too.
- **Bridges over the Ticino and the Po:** Ticino at Pavia (stone), Vigevano and the Novara to Vercelli road (wood); Po at
  Turin, Piacenza, Cremona, on the Pavia to Voghera road and on the Novara to Vercelli road (wood, or boat bridge). The
  generator gives a ferry wherever a village alone must cross; none was needed here.
- **Borders:** Bergamo and Brescia are Venetian in 1480 (the Adda is the frontier); the Duchy of Milan had Piacenza, Parma,
  Cremona. Roads cross borders; thread 5 draws the border.

## 10. Sockets I need

Proposal for the shared vocabulary (thread 4 owns the final list). Each is carried on a **hex edge** (the midpoint), both
hexes agree by construction because roads are routed over edges, not collapsed.

| Socket | Values | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `road` | 0 none, 1 track, 2 road, 3 highway | Edge quality. A hex with any road edge is a junction at its centre. |
| `crossing` | none, ford, bridge_wood, bridge_stone, ferry, pass | Valid only on a river edge (first four), a river edge with a great river (ferry), or a ridge edge (pass). A `pass` road ignores the ridge wall. |
| `quay` | 0 or 1 | On an edge whose other side is sea, lake or a navigable river; only if the land hex has a port. |

Hex attributes (not sockets, set by this thread, never constrained across edges): `settlement` 0 to 5, `capital`, `castle`
0 to 3, `landmark` (none, hospice, monastery, mine), plus derived tags for art: `site` (confluence, crossing, bay,
valley_mouth, pass_foot, lake_shore), the spill counts, the switchback flag. `port` is derived (a coast hex with a quay).

**What this adds to the vocabulary of the brief** (river, road, coast, ridge, valley floor...): road quality (4 values),
the crossing kinds, the quay. **What it does not need:** a "town" socket, because a settlement is a hex attribute.

### What I need from thread 1 (relief)

- Per hex: terrain kind (plain, hills, mountain, high), an elevation value 0 to 1, fertility (arable), an ore flag.
- A **valley** flag per hex with its two axis edges (the floor), so the road can follow it and the art can draw a ribbon.
- The **ridge** edge flag (already in the game) and a **slope step** between two hexes (for switchbacks).
- Passes as edge flags on the ridge, with a name, and the hex that is the saddle.
- Land touching the sea is hills at most (the Riviera strip): the mockup assumes it.

### What I need from thread 2 (water)

- River class per edge (stream, river, great river) **crossing at the edge midpoint**, so a bridge sits on the road's own
  edge socket. A river drawn as a free curve (mockup 07) must be built from those midpoints.
- A **confluence** flag, a **navigable** flag, and whether a river edge is **fordable** (shallow, no gorge).
- Lake and sea edges, a **bay** flag (a concave coast), shelter and depth for harbour quality, marsh and flood plain.
- The Po's tributaries: Ticino, Adda, Sesia, Dora Baltea, Tanaro, Oglio, Lambro (the data has only the Po; I drew them by hand).

### Role of WFC (for thread 4)

Settlements and roads are **not** collapsed by WFC. They are a placement and routing pass that runs after relief and water,
because WFC is local and cannot promise that Milan connects to Genoa across 8 hexes and a pass. After the pass, every hex's
sockets are known; WFC (or a lookup) then only picks the **tile variant** from the library that fits the sockets (thread
5). Consistency across edges comes free from routing on edges.

## 11. How this differs from the Python example

| | Python v0.2.2 | This design |
|---|---|---|
| Road data | a set of hexes (`world.roads`), one kind | quality 0 to 3 on every edge |
| Who connects | each country's towns to its network, capitals to near capitals, inside one country | by rank graph, across borders; passes, coasts, valleys |
| Relief | terrain move cost, no valley idea | valley floors cheap, ridges closed except passes, switchbacks drawn |
| Bridges | drawn when a road meets a river, no kind | ford, wood, stone, ferry by river size and place level |
| Ports, quays | a building in a coastal town | quay edge, lake and river landings |

| Villages | names only | placed by a site score and spacing |
| Passes | ridge exceptions only | hospice, track, valley approaches, a named saddle |
| Scale | 55 km hex | 28 km recommended |

## 12. Player and AI

- **Player:** nothing new to learn: roads and bridges are read from the picture, the hover card names the place, level,
  castle, and the road quality of an edge. Game effects (movement cost, supply, trade route capacity) are for the systems
  designer; the numbers above are starting values.
- **AI:** it paths over the same road graph (cost by quality), so it prefers highways and fears ferries and passes in
  winter (the existing pass closure). New settlements (colonies, founded towns) use the same **site score and spacing** as
  the generator, and the player can be shown "good site" hexes from it. The AI never places a road in the first version.
- **Determinism:** the whole pass is seeded through `core/rng.gd`; the same seed gives the same network. Fixed data plus
  the seed is the only input.

## 13. UI

Closest zoom: the level plates, gates, quays and bridges of `03b`; name plates with level dots and a crown for capitals
(from the godot-look README, direction A). Middle zoom: roads as coloured lines, bridges and ferries as small icons,
passes in italics, hospices. Far zoom: settlements as plates, roads only above quality 2. A "roads" map mode (the existing

## 14. Interactions

- **Economy:** settlement level maps to population and slots; ports and river landings anchor trade routes (Python
  `build_routes` over sea distance); road quality can scale route capacity and supply range.
- **War:** bridges, passes and ferries are chokepoints; a standalone castle covers each; the ridge-edge rule stays; winter
  closes passes (existing); blockading a port cuts its quay.
- **Tech:** later ideas can raise road quality by one step or let a ferry become a bridge. Not in the first version.
- **Debt:** none. **Diplomacy:** roads cross borders, so a border can be closed in a later rule.
- **Threads:** 1 (relief inputs), 2 (water inputs), 4 (sockets, ordering), 5 (tile variants for each level, quality, crossing).

## 15. Risks

- **Scale (section 4):** at 55 km the whole design degrades into satellite names. Mitigation: question 1.
- **Roads that look drawn by a ruler:** straight stubs from edge midpoints. Mitigation: pairs of opposite edges draw as a
  curve; art adds wobble; the real tile variants (thread 5).
- **Exploits and snowballing:** a highway trunk through the capital speeds the owner's armies; roads cross borders so the
  enemy gets them too. Mitigation: roads are static in the first version; war's chokepoint castles and ferries are the
  counter; the systems designer tests the march costs.
- **Rank-based bridges** can contradict history (Pavia and the Po). Mitigation: authored overrides.
- **Density:** a village every 2 hexes on the plain, at 122,000 hexes, is many objects; the spill layer must be drawn
  per-viewport and baked, not stored.
- **Data gaps:** the names table misses Como, Monza, Varese, Susa, Brig, Airolo. Mitigation: authored notable places.

## 16. Smallest first version

1. Fixed skeleton only: the 102 cities plus the 24 named passes plus hospices; levels from size; snap rule.
2. Generated villages and hamlets by site score and spacing, from `placenames` ranks.
3. The road graph with only rules 1 to 5 (no coast or valley rules), routed by cost, quality from rank only, crossings by
   the table.
4. Three edge sockets: `road`, `crossing`, `quay`. One tile art per level and per quality.

This already gives Milan to Genoa over a bridge and a pass, and the three Alpine routes, readable in one frame.

## Appendix A. Technical notes (for the builder)

### Data files (proposed, all editable without code)

`godot/data/settlement_levels.json`: `LEVEL_FROM_SIZE` (the size bands), `LEVEL_FROM_RANK` (rank bands),
`SPACING` per level, `SPILL` (patches per ring per level), `SITE_SCORE` (the table of section 6), `CASTLE_FROM_SIZE`.
`godot/data/roads.json`: `ROAD_COST` (the section 8 table), `LINK_RANK` (which link kind gives which quality), `TRAFFIC_UP`
(4), `QUALITY_CAP_BY_GROUND`, `CROSSING_RULES` (the bridge table), `MOVE_FACTOR` per quality (starting values only).
`godot/data/notable_places.json`: authored entries `{name, lon, lat, level, castle, landmark, note}` and crossing overrides.
Constants are named; none in code.

### Pipeline order (after relief and water)

1. Snap and level the fixed cities and notable places. 2. Score sites; place generated settlements in descending score, with
spacing. 3. Place landmarks (hospices on pass hexes, monasteries, mines) and standalone castles. 4. Build the link list
(section 8). 5. Route links in descending quality (highways first so they set the trunks), updating edge costs, then
compute traffic and final quality. 6. Choose crossing kinds. 7. Derive the quay edges and the spill layer. 8. Emit the
sockets. All randomness through `core/rng.gd` with a sub-stream per step so adding a step does not change the others.

### Edge model

Roads are stored per undirected edge (a pair of hexes) as one byte: bits 0 to 1 quality, bits 2 to 4 crossing kind, bit 5
quay. Both hexes read the same byte, so they can never disagree. A hex's road junction is implied by any road edge. Pass edges
reuse the game's `pass_edges`; this thread only adds a road on them.

### Evidence and reproducibility

The mockups were produced by a scratch script (hand relief stand-in, the model above, pygame drawing), not stored in the
repo. Measured: 180 hexes, 37 settlements, 58 links all routed, road edges 14 highway, 27 road, 24 track; 17 river crossings
found, of which 2 stone bridges, 14 wooden bridges, 1 ford, 0 ferries (the Po needed none because a town sits at each
crossing; a ferry is drawn in the legend and appears when a village alone must cross). At `SCALE 2` 43 places fall in 25
hexes, at `SCALE 4` in 38 (6 shared pairs: Milan and Monza, Como and Varese, Novara and Vercelli, Tortona and Voghera, Ivrea
and Biella, Albenga and Imperia).
