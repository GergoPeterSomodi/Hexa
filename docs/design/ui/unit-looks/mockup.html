Unit rework: how units look and how you pick them

Mockup for the owner's choice, made for the recruitment queue (issue 463), which now includes the unit rework. The owner's limit: looks and selection only. That covers unit art, unit cards, and clicking a unit apart from its tile. No new kinds of unit, and no changes to combat or army rules.

Recommendation: option D, the owner's direction: the old game's figures on a country-coloured disc, with far more detail, plus the fan picker for stacks. Build it with two detail levels (full detail on cards and close up, a simpler version at normal zoom) and the old round token with a flag and age dots when zoomed far out. It is the largest build of the four. If that is too much for this feature, option A is the small fallback and reads best when small. The nine old types that come next are previewed in sheet 2b. Costs are at the end of the page.

1. How units look today

Drawn from the code. These are the four 32 px figures of the baked kit (fig_infantry, fig_missile, fig_cavalry, fig_artillery) and the token from army_token.gd. The token has a 40×34 hex plate tinted in the country's colour, a 26 px figure, a 46×22 brass badge with the men, a move-points bar, a 16 px state icon, and a square ink-and-gold ring when selected. A country has one token per hex. Its figure is the kind with the most men.

Summer tiles at 100%: Genoa (yours) pikemen, crossbowmen, knights, bombards, then Milan and Venice (foreign)
Parchment map at 33%: tokens keep their screen size, so they are bigger than a hex
On cards (Army tab tile, 28 px): today the code puts a brass pike or crossbow mark over two of the figures because they look alike at this size

What is wrong today

  • Pikemen and crossbowmen look alike: both are a cream man in a kettle hat. At 26 px only the crossbow's stave tells them apart. The cards needed an extra mark (FIGURE_MARKS) to fix it.
  • Knights and bombards are fine up close, but at 33% all four become small brown blobs on a coloured hexagon.
  • Own and foreign tokens differ only by the move-points bar. Milan and Venice are both teal, and the colour-blind palette does not help on a small plate.
  • The token shows one kind only. A mixed army looks like a single-kind army.
  • Two countries on one hex share the exact same spot (both tokens sit at the hex centre), so one hides the other.
  • The selection ring is a square round the token's click box, unlike the round and hex shapes used elsewhere on the map.

2. Four options for the unit art

Each option shows the four kinds, yours and foreign, on summer and winter tiles at 100%, on the parchment map at 33%, and at a far zoom. It then shows the same kinds as they appear on cards. Use Simulate vision above to check them without colour. Shape alone should still tell the kinds apart, and own from foreign.

2b. Next feature: the old unit types return (in style D)

The owner decided that the old game's nine types (three branches over three ages) come back as the feature after this one. They are not part of this rework, which keeps today's four units. This sheet checks that D leaves room for all nine to read apart. Each age changes the silhouette, not just the colour, because several old figures looked alike. Infantry hats go kettle, crest, broad brim, and weapons go upright pike, raised gun, shouldered musket with a fork. Cavalry goes sword and barding, then a long lance, then a black rider with a pistol. Guns go fat on a bed, then long on big wheels, then short with a gunner. Each cell shows full detail, the real token size on the map, and the far token with its age dots. Crossbowmen are today's fourth kind and are not in the old line-up; they keep their own figure.

3. Map states

Shown in the option picked at the top (A), at 100% zoom. Numbers refer to the list below the map.

    4. Try selection: unit or tile, and picking from a stack

    Click a token to select that army (gold halo; the army panel opens). Click anywhere else on a hex to select the tile (gold hex ring; the tile window opens). Savona holds three of your armies. The left map does what the game does today. The right map adds the fan picker. Hover a token to see its card. A foreign token is not a unit you can command: a click on it selects its tile, and the tile window's Army tab lists the army.

    Today: each click on a stack takes the next army

    Good: one click and you have an army, and the keys you know stay the same (Tab goes to the next army that needs orders). Bad: you cannot see which armies are in the stack or how big each is until you have clicked through them all.

    Fan picker (recommended): the stack opens

    The first click on a stack selects the top army, as today, and also fans every army out above the hex, each with its own sign and men. Click one to take it. A second click on the stack still steps to the next army, and Esc or a click elsewhere closes the fan. A single army never fans out.

    A third way, spreading the stack apart while the mouse is over it, was considered and left out. Tokens would jump under the pointer and overlap the next hex. It is not clearly better.

    5. Cards: Army tab with the queue, army panel, hover cards

    Same frames, wood and gold as today. Only the unit pictures change (shown in option A) and the Raise button becomes the queue stepper from issue 463. Click the plus and minus buttons to try the queue: up to 5 companies, and one trains each season.

    6. Recommendation and cost

    Option D, the old game's figures with more detail, with the fan picker. It is what the owner asked for, and it reads as soldiers rather than counters. Each kind has an outline no other kind shares: upright pikes, a pavise and level crossbows, a horse, a low gun. The nine types of the next feature fit the same family (sheet 2b). Detail is used only where it can be seen: cards and close zoom get full detail, normal zoom gets a simplified figure, and far zoom gets the round token. The cost is the largest of the four, mostly in drawing ten figures twice in the baker.
    The fallback is option A, "Shield and sign". It reads best at every size for the least work. Each kind has one bold sign, and the four signs share no outline: three spikes, a T with a bow, a horse head, a wheel with a barrel. They read on snow, on grass and on parchment because they sit on a pale disc, not on the ground. Your own armies get a gold rim and the move-points bar; foreign ones get a dark iron rim and no bar. That difference shows in grey too. A mixed army shows its other kinds as small discs, so you can see what it holds at a glance. The same signs go on the cards, so the extra pike and crossbow marks can go.

    Questions for the owner

    1. Which look: D (the old figures, more detail, Large) or A (shield and sign, Small)? (Recommended: D, as asked. A if this feature must stay small. B and C drop out.)
    2. Stacks: add the fan picker, or keep only the click cycle? (Recommended: add the fan; the cycle and Tab stay.)
    3. Should a mixed army show its other kinds on the token (small discs), or only its main kind, as today? (Recommended: show them.)
    4. In D, which figure is the main one for a mixed army? The kind with the most men, as today, with the other kinds as small discs. (Recommended: as today.)
    5. Foreign armies you can see: their main kind and men only, as today, or their whole mix? (Recommended: as today. The fog rules give a foreign army's men but not its companies.)

    What this page cannot show exactly

    • The ground is simple flat hexes, not the game's tile art or parchment shader. The town is a few houses, not the game's settlement pictures.
    • Today's plate, badge and ring follow the code's sizes and colours, but the baked plate's soft shadow and gilt shading are approximated.
    • The page uses system fonts, not the game's Alegreya fonts.
    • D's two-tone shading here uses a hard-stop gradient per part. The baker would draw a lit half and a shaded half per part, which looks the same. Today's kit has no such helper yet.
    • Option art is a sketch made in the browser. The final art would be drawn in the same signed-distance baker as today's kit, at 1x and 2x.
