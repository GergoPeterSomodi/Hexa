# Hexa architecture review (draft for the owner)

Technical director, 2026-11-12. Code: `master` at v0.1.7. Analysis only: no repo code was changed.
Every number below comes from the scripts next to this file (`analyse.py`, `surface.py`, `sections.py`,
`savesize.py`); their raw output is in `measurements.txt`, `surface.txt`, `sections.txt`, `savesize.txt`, and the
import graph is in `imports.dot` (Graphviz).

---

## Executive summary

**The verdict: the code is not spaghetti yet, but it is heading there, in three places.** The good news first:
the rules are already pygame-free (checked: no world, rules, AI, geo, WFC or saves module imports pygame), the UI
never touches a private world attribute, the `*_check`/action contract is enforced by tests, and there is a strong
safety net (about 800 tests, soak, perf, save-compat, golden screenshots). That net is what makes a safe
refactor possible.

The three problems:

1. **`CampaignWorld` is a god class.** It is 3,203 lines, with 222 methods and 38 attributes, and it holds 19
   areas: map building, passes, markets, the economy, debt, tech, units, supply, movement, war and saves. Its
   section headings no longer match what is under them. For example, the economy's income, debt and growth code
   sits under "what the techs change". The app uses 86 different world methods, and the AI uses 45.
2. **The app does everything.** `CampaignApp` is 1,566 lines and mixes in `GameMenu` and `TechScreen` (the two
   import each other). It owns the season loop, input, every panel and a 91-line `press()` dispatcher. The season
   loop exists three times: in the app, in the test helpers and in `tools/save_compat.py`, and the copies already
   differ (only the app calls `settle_preview`). Nothing announces events. The app finds out about new wars by
   comparing war lists from before and after the turn.
3. **The art and render code repeats itself.** Six hand-made drawing classes (`Canvas` or `Pen`) re-implement
   `poly`, `line`, `circle`, `stamp`, `shadow` and the hex mask in five art modules. There are about 25 module-level
   caches, and 19 of them are never emptied. The budget logic is written twice, in mapview and in landscape.
   There are six button implementations, and `_fit` (text fitting) exists three times.

**The recommendation: a five-phase refactor in about 30 small PRs.** None of them changes behaviour, and each
one is proved by a behaviour oracle (the same world digest before and after), save-compat, soak, perf and
pixel-identical goldens:

- **Phase 0, safety net:** the behaviour oracle and architecture guard tests (2 PRs).
- **Phase 1, event bus and game manager:** this unblocks the floating text, action log and resource bar (4 PRs).
- **Phase 2, UI kit and panels:** this unblocks the UI clean-up (5 PRs).
- **Phase 3, systems out of `CampaignWorld`:** one module per system behind a thin facade, with saves unchanged
  (about 13 PRs).
- **Phase 4, shared render cache, frame budget and drawing primitives** (4 PRs).

Conquest and Colonisation stay frozen. Measured, they share very little code with the campaign.

**Decisions for the owner** (details in section 6):

- **D1.** How systems are split: function modules with a facade (recommended), mixins, or system objects that
  hold their own state.
- **D2.** Whether to freeze the two older modes.
- **D3.** Whether to stop saving derived caches. Saves would be 11% smaller, and this is compatible.
- **D4.** Whether feature work waits for its system's extraction (battle system after the war extraction).
- **D5.** Whether to accept that new saves will not load in older builds.

### 1.5 Hot spots: hidden coupling, state and caches

1. **The season loop is in the app and is copied.**
   - `CampaignApp.end_turn` runs draw and flip, then `settle_preview`, the AI for each non-player country,
     `end_round`, order reminders, `refresh`, autosave, war announcements and the research toast.
   - The same loop is written again in `tests/support/worlds.py:play_seasons` and
     `tools/save_compat.py:season`. Those two copies skip `settle_preview`.
   - Fifteen `end_round()` calls are spread over 10 files.
2. **There are no events.**
   - The only channel is `world.message(text)`: a list of strings for the player only, capped at 80, with 34 call
     sites.
   - The app detects wars by diffing `_war_keys()` before and after the turn.
   - The resource bar, floating text and action log all need structured events, with a kind, a place, a country
     and an amount.
3. **Drawing changes the world.** `CampaignApp.draw()` calls `world.ensure_markets()` every frame, which can
   rebuild the markets and routes. It works, but it is a hidden write in a read path.
4. **The UI and AI write state directly**, not through actions:
   - the app sets `world.player`, `world.countries[tag].memory` and `unit.goal` (6 sites)
   - the AI sets `country.tax` directly, instead of `set_tax`
5. **The cache invalidation is manual.**
   - `world.version` is bumped by `changed()` at 13 sites in the world and 1 in the app.
   - mapview reads it at 8 sites, landscape at 3.
   - A mutation that forgets `changed()` leaves a stale map. Nothing checks for that today (see step 0.2).
6. **There are about 25 module-level caches in 10 modules.**
   - **Bounded:** landscape's ground and object LRUs (3,000 and 6,000 entries), the `lru_cache`s in mapview
     (2,048 entries) and goodsobjects (512), and the world's `_SNAPSHOTS` and `_RANDOM_GEOS` (2 random worlds).
   - **Never emptied** (no `pop`, `clear` or `del` was found), 19 in all: `art._CACHE`, `wfctiles._CACHE`,
     `_MASKS`, `_SPRITES`, `_SHADOWS`, `_SPARE` and `_BASES`, `goodsart._SPR` and `_MASK`, `life._OBJ_CACHE`
     and `_MASKS`, landscape's `_OB`, `_PIECE` and `_BLOBS`, `nature._BUILD` and its three
     `lru_cache(maxsize=None)`s, and `techscreen._fonts`.
   - Memory issue #29 lives here. Nothing can report or clear all of them together.
7. **The frame budget is written twice.** mapview has `LAND_BUILD_MS`, `PREFETCH_*`, `LAND_IDLE_MS`,
   `LAND_STILL_MS`, `_gen_budget` and `_frame_deadline`, with `perf_counter` at about 20 sites. landscape has
   its own LRU helper.
8. **Saves contain caches.**
   - `save_state()` pickles the whole `__dict__`, including `_owned`, `_sea_cache`, `_neighbour_cache`,
     `_pass_cache` and `_settled`.
   - Measured on Europe after 4 AI seasons: the save is **8.0 MiB**, and **7.2 MiB** without the derived caches
     (-11%). Pickling takes 50 ms.
   - The save also depends on every attribute name and every dataclass's module path, which is why any move
     needs care (section 3.7).
9. **The mixins share `self`.** `CampaignApp(GameMenu, TechScreen)`: all three read and write the same
   `self.ui`, `self.screen`, fonts and buttons. So a menu or tech-screen change can break the map screen, and
   the reverse.

## 2. Duplication

### 2.1 Across the three modes

| Pair | Duplicated 6-line windows | Same-named functions |
|---|---:|---|
| colony/app.py and play.py (Conquest App) | 9 | handle, press, refresh, end_turn |
| colony/render.py and render.py | 8 (`button_rects` twice) | draw, sprite |

| campaign and Conquest or Colonisation | 0 | (same names only: `found_check`, `build_check`, `cost_field`... with different rules) |

**The finding:** the campaign shares almost no code with the other two modes. Its rules, render path (mapview,
art, landscape) and app are all its own, and only `hexgrid` is common. The real duplication between modes is
inside the older pair (Conquest and Colonisation, about 3,000 lines together). It is small and stable.
**Recommendation: don't merge the modes.** Freeze Conquest and Colonisation (bug fixes only), and if they come
back, rebuild them later on the campaign engine, as the roadmap's "focus on Age of Discovery" note suggests.
They only pick up the new UI kit and theme when they are touched anyway.


### 3.1 The game manager (`campaign/game.py`, pygame-free)

- **It owns:**
  - the world
  - the event bus
  - the player tag
  - the autosave policy
  - `end_season()`, in a fixed order: `settle_preview`, then AI for each non-player country, `end_round`
    (which runs the phases in `systems/season.py`), order reminders, autosave, and finally `season_ended`
    emitted
  - `new_game(map, seed, tag)`, `load(slot)` and `save(slot)`
- **The app keeps** drawing the busy frame before calling `end_season()`, then refreshing, and showing the
  events.
- **The three loop copies become one:**
  - `tests/support/worlds.play_seasons` calls the manager.
  - `tools/save_compat.py` keeps its own loop on purpose, because it must run against old checkouts that have no
    manager.
- **The screen stack** (tech screen, Esc menu, the idea modal, later the battle screen) replaces the two mixins. Each
  screen has `handle_event(ctx, event)`, `draw(ctx)` and `update(ctx, dt)`, and they share one ctx instead of `self`.
  *As built (step 2.5):* the stack is the window's (`CampaignApp.screens`, `campaign/screens/`), not the manager's. A
  screen draws with pygame and answers to the window's mouse, fonts and size, and the manager had to stay pygame-free so
  that tests, tools and the AI play without a window. The ctx is the app itself, used through its public attributes and
  methods only (the list is in `screens/__init__.py`), not a separate `UiContext` object: a second object would have
  copied the app's 15 or so attributes for no gain.

### 3.2 Systems as modules

- **The recommended form (option A, see D1):** plain functions taking `world`, such as
  `trade.send_merchant(world, tag, route)`.
  - `CampaignWorld` keeps one-line facade methods (`def send_merchant(self, *a, **k): return
    trade.send_merchant(self, *a, **k)`).
  - Every caller keeps working unchanged: the app's 86 methods, the AI's 45, tests, tools and
    `save_compat`'s public API.
- **Each system module states which world attributes it owns**, in a header such as
  `OWNS = ("markets", "routes", "_sea_cache", "_markets_dirty")`. A guard test (step 0.2) checks that a system
  assigns only its own attributes.
- **No new attribute moves** into a system object, so the pickle layout does not change.
- **Facades can be retired later**, call site by call site, as code is touched. The rule: new code calls the
  system, and old code may keep calling the facade.

### 3.3 The event bus (`hexa/events.py`)

- **An event:** `Event(kind, tag=None, pos=None, data=None)`, a frozen dataclass.
- **The bus:** `emit(kind, **fields)` appends to a queue. The UI calls `drain()` once a frame and dispatches to
  `subscribe(kind, handler)` handlers. Tests read `bus.history`.
- **It is attached as a transient world attribute.** `world.bus` is excluded from `save_state()`, in the same way
  as `geo`, and a fresh one is set in `_restore` and `__init__`. Without a subscriber it only records (bounded).
- **Kinds to start with:** `province_captured`, `siege_started`, `battle`, `unit_raised`, `unit_lost`,
  `unit_deserted`, `war_declared`, `peace_made`, `pass_closed`, `pass_opened`, `debt_seized`, `bankrupt`,
  `tech_researched`, `era_reached`, `colony_founded`, `money` (amount, reason, place: this is the resource bar's
  "one-off items") and `message` (the existing log line).
- **Two rules:**
  - Handlers never change the world. Events are notifications, so the AI and the season results stay
    deterministic. The behaviour oracle checks this.
  - `world.message()` stays, so the log and its save format are unchanged. It also emits `message`.
- **It feeds:** the floating text (events with a `pos`), the action log (all events, with filters by kind
  group), the resource bar (`money` events plus a start-of-season snapshot), and the end-of-turn reminders.

### 3.4 The UI kit (`hexa/ui/`)

- **It follows `docs/design/ui/README.md` section 1:** `Button(kind, state, icon, detail, size)`, `Panel`
  (a header band, tabs, a scroll offset for each tab, a footer that stays put), `Row`, `Tooltip`/hover card,
  `Modal`, and the text helpers `fit` and `wrap`.
- **It stays immediate-mode**, like today: you call `kit.button(...)` while drawing, it registers a hit box, and
  `press()` gets the action. That keeps the port mechanical.
- **First it reproduces today's look exactly** (a "classic" style), so the goldens are unchanged. Then the owner
  approves the new style (proposal 01) and switches it in a separate PR that regenerates the goldens.
- **`theme.py`** holds the colour tokens (today's `TEXT`, `MUTED`, `GOLD`...) plus the proposal's `PANEL_HEAD`,
  `CARD_BG`, `WARN_C` and `INFO_C`, and one font loader.

### 3.5 A shared render cache and budget helper (`campaign/render/`)

- **`BoundedCache(name, max_entries, max_bytes=None)`:** an LRU with hit, miss and size statistics, registered
  in one `CACHES` registry. It gives the perf memory row (#29) a real breakdown, and one `clear_all()` for a
  display-mode change.
- **`FrameBudget(ms)`:** `start()`, `fits(expected_ms)`, `spend()` and `left()`. It replaces mapview's
  deadlines and landscape's `_lru_put`, with the same constants (`LAND_BUILD_MS`, `PREFETCH_*`).
- **`draw.py`:** one `Canvas` with `poly`, `line(s)`, `circle`, `oval`, `stamp`, `shadow`, `hex_mask`,
  `in_poly`, `mix` and `rng`, used by goodsart, goodsobjects, wfctiles, nature, landmarks and life.

### 3.6 Data

The numbers stay in `campaign/data.py`. The README tables mirror it, as they do now. The systems import from
`data`, never the other way round. `world.py`'s 60-name `from .data import (...)` block shrinks as the systems
take their own imports.

### 3.7 How saves stay compatible

1. **No attribute renames and no new attributes** in Phases 0-4, so `WORLD_DEFAULTS`, `WORLD_REQUIRED` and
   `_migrate` do not change. The only exception is the transient `bus`, which is excluded from `save_state()`.
2. **Pickle finds classes by module path** (`hexa.campaign.world.Province`). When the dataclasses move to
   `state.py`, `world.py` re-exports them, so old saves still load. New saves will name
   `hexa.campaign.state.Province`, so they won't load in v0.1.7 or older builds. This is the usual direction and
   is allowed by the `VERSION` policy (D5).
   - The alternative is to set `Province.__module__ = "hexa.campaign.world"` after the move, which keeps saves
     byte-compatible both ways. This is cheap, so it is recommended for this one step.
3. **`CampaignWorld` keeps its name and module.** Systems are functions, not attributes, so nothing new is
   pickled.
4. **Optional (D3):** drop the derived caches (`_owned`, `_castle_list`, `_sea_cache`, `_neighbour_cache`,
   `_pass_cache`, `_settled`) from `save_state()`. They are already in `WORLD_DEFAULTS` and are rebuilt on
   demand, so old saves that carry them still load. This needs no `VERSION` bump. It is one PR, proved by
   `save_compat` both ways.
5. **Every PR that touches `hexa/campaign/` already runs the CI save-compat job.** Locally, run
   `tools/save_compat.py write <base> ...` then `check`.

## 4. The step-by-step plan

**The rule for every step:** one PR, no behaviour change, and no gameplay or art change in the same commit. The
full suite, the ui suite and the save-compat job are green. Each step also passes the behaviour oracle
(step 0.1). Sizes are lines moved (M) or new (N), measured from the current files.

**Proof kit used below:**

- **ORACLE:** the same campaign digests as the base checkout, for Europe 1480 over 12 seasons, random seeds 7
  and 11, and the colony and conquest digests.
  - Measured today: `digest_run campaign 1480 --turns 12` gives `e26ad1a6...` in 3-4 s.
  - It is stable across two runs and across `PYTHONHASHSEED` 0 and 1.
- **SC:** save_compat write (base) and check (branch).
- **SOAK:** `tools.soak --quick` (about 15 s).
- **PERF:** `tools.perf`, the affected metrics within the soft budget.
- **GOLD:** the 15 goldens, sha-identical (not just within tolerance).

## 5. Rules to adopt (proposed CLAUDE.md additions)

```markdown
### Structure rules (from the 2026 architecture review)
- **Layers, imports point down only:** data -> state -> systems -> world (facade) -> game manager -> screens/panels
  -> app. Systems take `world` as a parameter and never import world.py, ui, render or pygame. Render and
  panels read the world through public methods only and never assign to world or state objects.
- **Player actions:** a new action is `x_check(world, ...)`/`x(world, ...)` in its system module, with a facade
  on CampaignWorld and a pair in tests/support/agents.py. The UI and the AI change state only through actions.
- **One owner per attribute:** each system module lists the world attributes it writes in `OWNS`;
  tests/test_architecture.py enforces it. A new attribute goes in `WORLD_DEFAULTS` (or a dataclass default) in
  the same PR. Derived caches are listed in `WORLD_CACHES` and are not saved.
- **Events, not polling:** a system that changes something the player should notice calls
  `world.bus.emit(kind, ...)`. Event handlers never change the world. The bus is never saved.
- **Season order** lives in campaign/systems/season.py `PHASES` and nowhere else; the game manager runs it.
- **Size limits:** a module over 800 lines needs a reason in its docstring; over 1,200 it is split. A function
  over 60 lines needs a reason; over 100 it is split (art recipes and map generation: 150). Today's offenders
  are allowlisted in tests/test_architecture.py and may not grow.
- **No cross-module `_private` use** outside tests. Tests prefer public functions of system modules.
- **UI:** buttons, panels, rows, tooltips and modals come from hexa/ui/kit.py, colours and fonts from
  hexa/ui/theme.py. No new hand-drawn button.
- **Render caches** are `render.cache.BoundedCache` instances, registered by name. No new module-level dict
  cache. Time-sliced work uses `render.budget.FrameBudget`.
- **Refactor PRs** change no behaviour: label them `refactor`; they must pass tools/same_behaviour.py,
  save-compat and sha-identical goldens. Never mix a refactor and a gameplay change in one commit.
- **Where new features go:** rules -> campaign/systems/<name>.py (+ numbers in data.py, + README table);
  panel -> campaign/panels/; screen -> campaign/screens/; art -> its art module using render/draw.py.
```

The "Campaign internals" section of CLAUDE.md also needs updating as each phase lands: the turn flow goes to the
manager, and the `world.changed()` rule moves into the systems.

## 6. Decisions for the owner

**Approved by the owner on 2026-11-12: all six recommended choices** (D1 function modules + facade, D2 freeze Conquest and Colonisation, D3 stop saving derived caches, D4 interleave with feature rounds, D5 pin dataclass `__module__`, D6 queued events). Phase 0 starts first.

**D1. How to split `CampaignWorld`:**

- **A (recommended):** function modules plus a facade. Dependencies are explicit (a `world` parameter), the save
  format is unchanged, and the call sites are unchanged. The cost is about 150 one-line facade methods for a
  while.
- **B:** mixins (`CampaignWorld(TradeMixin, WarMixin...)`). This is the smallest diff: methods move verbatim. But
  every mixin still sees all of `self`, so it splits the file, not the design.
- **C:** system objects that hold their own state (`world.trade.markets`). This is the cleanest ownership, but it
  changes the pickle layout, needs `_migrate` work and probably a `VERSION` bump. Do it later if ever, system by
  system.

**D2. Conquest and Colonisation:**

- **A (recommended):** freeze them, fixing bugs only. They share only `hexgrid` with the campaign.
- **B:** port them to the UI kit as well (+2 PRs).
- **C:** remove them now, which the roadmap is already considering.

**D3. Derived caches in saves:**

- **A (recommended):** stop saving them. The save goes from 8.0 to 7.2 MiB, it is compatible, and it is one PR.
- **B:** leave them as they are.

**D4. Pace against features:**

- **A (recommended):** interleave. The refactor PRs land between bug-fix and feature rounds, and a feature waits
  only for its own system: the battle system after 3.12, debt after 3.9, the floating text, action log and
  resource bar after 1.3 and 2.2.
- **B:** a refactor sprint with a feature freeze (about 30 PRs).

**D5. Pickled class paths when the dataclasses move (3.1):**

- **A (recommended):** pin `__module__` so saves stay two-way compatible.
- **B:** re-export only. New saves won't open in older builds.

**D6. The event bus delivery:**

- **A (recommended):** queued, drained once a frame by the UI, with a history for tests.
- **B:** synchronous callbacks. This is simpler, but a slow handler then runs inside the season loop.

## 7. Risks and what is not finished

- **Merge conflicts.** world.py and app.py are touched by almost every feature. Each move PR should be merged
  quickly, between rounds. Open contributor PRs that touch these files should land first, or be rebased with
  help.
- **Hidden behaviour in the order of things.** Iteration order over `countries` and `provinces`, and RNG draws,
  must not change. The ORACLE (same digest) is the defence. It was checked to be deterministic across processes
  and hash seeds, but only for 12 Europe seasons in this review. Step 0.1 should add the random maps and a longer
  run.
- **The goldens run only on Linux CI.** A render refactor can't be proved pixel-identical on a Windows
  workstation. Step 4.4 adds a platform-independent sprite-hash test for exactly this.
- **Performance.** A facade adds one Python call per world method. The hot ones (`province`, `units_at`, `bonus`,
  `has`, `edge_blocked`, step costs) stay on the world as real methods. PERF on `ai_season` and
  `ai_season_late` will show any cost.
- **What was not measured:**
  - per-function runtime profiles (perf covers the totals only)
  - memory per cache (step 4.2 adds it)
  - whether every mutation that matters calls `world.changed()` (step 0.2 adds the check)
  - The duplicate detection is exact-match 6-line windows, so it under-counts near-duplicates (the art
    primitives were found by name instead).
- **The baseline** on this checkout: 790 tests green and perf within budget (section 8). `tests/ui` and the goldens
  were not run in this review.
